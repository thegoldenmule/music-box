# Kid Player (superseded draft)

**Status:** active

## Body
What the kid actually sees. It should feel like a real music app, not like a punishment. If it feels like a locked-down version of something better, kids will go find the better thing.

## Design rules

- **Artwork first.** Album art is how a kid who can't read finds things. Big tiles, not text rows.
- **No dead ends.** Blocked items shouldn't appear at all in level 1 or 2. A kid tapping something and being told no, repeatedly, is a bad experience and teaches them to look elsewhere.
- **But explain the ones they do hit.** Some blocks are unavoidable, like a blocked track inside an allowed playlist. Say something kind and true — "your grown-up hasn't added this one yet" — not an error code.
- **No links out.** No web views, no App Store links, no share sheets, no "open in Music." Required if we go Kids Category, sensible either way.
- **No upsell.** Nothing about subscriptions or purchases on the kid's side. Any of that belongs behind the parental gate.

## What's on screen

- **Home.** In level 1, this is the parent's allow list laid out as tiles — that's the whole app for a young kid. In levels 2 and 3, it's a starting point with recent plays and favorites.
- **Search.** Hidden entirely in level 1. In levels 2 and 3, a plain search field, filtered through the policy engine. Voice search matters here for pre-readers — see the research list.
- **Browse.** Tap an artist, see their albums. Tap an album, see its tracks. Filtered the same way.
- **Now playing.** Big artwork, and only the controls the license requires: play, pause, skip. No shuffle-into-the-unknown, no autoplay past the queue.

## Getting into and out of kid mode

The app should launch straight into the kid's player, not a chooser. Getting to the admin takes a deliberate gesture plus a passcode. If a device is shared between two kids, switching kids is also a parent action — otherwise the 12-year-old's profile is one tap away for the 5-year-old.

What we cannot do from inside this screen is stop a kid from leaving the app entirely. See Keeping Kids In The App (superseded draft).

## Mac differences

The Mac has no equivalent of Guided Access, no Family Controls, and a kid can always Command-Tab away. A Mac build is realistically a nice player with reporting, not a sandbox. Worth building, worth being honest about.
